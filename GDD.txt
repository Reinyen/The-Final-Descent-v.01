1. VISION & HIGH-LEVEL OVERVIEW
1.1 Core Concept

The Final Descent is a single-player, party-based, turn-based roguelike focused on tension, sacrifice, and legacy.

The player controls three members of a six-person expedition known as The Eleventh. The mission is to descend through a procedurally generated dungeon, survive long enough to reach its heart, and leave behind a magical record of their journey. The run always ends in retreat or death; the “victory” state is successfully preserving the party’s story, not killing a final boss.

There is no traditional final boss kill condition. Instead, the final encounter is unwinnable by design, and the goal is to endure until a scripted retreat can be executed.

1.2 Themes & Emotional Tone

Duty over survival: Characters know they will not return; their duty is to gather knowledge and seal the threat.

Legacy against oblivion: Runs are judged by the message left behind and the story the player constructs.

Pressure and anxiety: Systems (limited SP, dangerous enemies, unpredictable action pools) are tuned to keep the player under constant, but fair, stress.

The atmosphere is low fantasy with occult and temporal elements. Visual tone is muted, with sharp highlights during key actions and combos.

1.3 Target Experience & Run Length

For version 1.0:

A complete run consists of 4 dungeon floors.

Each floor has:

One Start room.

One Exit room.

A connected graph of intermediate rooms (5–10 per floor).

The party’s Level is always equal to the current floor number (1–4). Levels beyond 4 are reserved for future content.

Each full run should feel compact but meaningful: the goal is to get players quickly to interesting decisions and emotional beats, rather than long grinds.

2. CORE GAME STRUCTURE
2.1 Descent Loop (Macro)

The Descent Loop describes progression across floors.

Party Selection

At the start of a run, the game randomly selects 3 characters from the 6 members of The Eleventh.

In 1.0, party selection is fully random; future versions may add drafting or rerolls.

Enter Floor N

A room graph for Floor N is generated with a guaranteed traversable path from Start room to Exit room.

Floor number equals party Level.

Explore Rooms

From the Start room, the player chooses linked rooms to move into.

The map is a node graph (not strictly grid-based), shown as connected nodes.

Room Events
Each room can be:

Enemy – triggers a combat encounter.

Narrative – dialogue, lore, or decision-making events.

Resource – loot, healing, SP restoration, or special interactions.

Trap – hazards dealing damage or inflicting status.

Empty – safe rooms with no direct gameplay effect.

Floor 4 may additionally contain a special Antechamber and Final Chamber.

Resolve & Manage

After each event, the player manages party resources:

Hit Points (HP)

Shared Stamina Points (SP)

Status effects

Consumables (items)

Exit & Descent

Upon clearing the Exit room on a floor, the party descends to the next floor.

On entering a new floor, all living party members increase to the new Level value.

Final Floor & Endgame

Floor 4 leads eventually to the Final Chamber encounter.

The run ends in failure (total party incapacitation) or success (triggered retreat and legacy sequence).

2.2 Combat Loop (Micro)

Inside an Enemy room, the game switches to turn-based combat.

Combat Start

Enemies are instantiated according to the room’s encounter definition.

Party begins combat with 10 SP in the shared pool.

All statuses on party and enemies are applied as carried in from exploration.

Start of Round

Status effects resolve according to the Tide of Decay rules.

SP is generated using Fading Nerve (Section 4.2).

A new 4-action random pool is drawn (Four Fates Draw).

Player sees:

Current SP

The 4 random abilities

Always-available options (Basic Strike, Guard, Use Item).

Action Assignment Phase

Player selects actions for each conscious party member:

From the random pool (Standard/Special/Duo/Trio/Party actions).

From always-available actions.

Each selection consumes SP from the shared pool.

Each character may be assigned at most one action per round.

Duo and Trio actions consume the actions of all participants.

Initiative Phase

For every acting combatant, compute initiative as base SPD plus the selected ability’s speed modifier.

All combatants with assigned actions (party and enemies) are sorted into an initiative order.

Action Resolution Phase

Units act in order from highest to lowest initiative.

If a unit is incapacitated or otherwise unable to act when their turn arrives, their selected action is canceled for that round.

End of Round

Any unused SP carries over but is subject to Fading Nerve clamping.

Check win/loss conditions.

If battle continues, a new round begins at step 2.

3.2 Core Attributes

Every player character and enemy has the following core combat attributes:

HP (Hit Points)

Current health value.

When HP reaches 0 or below, the unit is incapacitated (KO) and cannot act.

CON (Conviction)

Represents mental and spiritual resilience.

Used in the Shared Blood formula to scale damage and healing.

SPD (Speed)

Determines how quickly units act within a round.

Combined with ability-specific speed modifiers to calculate initiative.

No other core numerical attributes (such as Strength or Intelligence) are required at version 1.0; everything is expressed via HP, CON, SPD, and statuses.

3.3 Level System & Level_Value Table

Party members’ Level is equal to the current floor number:

Floor 1 → Level 1

Floor 2 → Level 2

Floor 3 → Level 3

Floor 4 → Level 4

Levels affect the Conviction term in damage and healing formulas.

Level_Value is a per-level multiplier:

Level	Level_Value
1	0.5
2	0.6
3	0.7
4	0.8

For future expansion beyond Floor 4, Level_Value can continue to increase by 0.1 per level as needed.

There is no separate experience system in 1.0: leveling is entirely tied to floor progression.

4. CORE COMBAT SYSTEMS
4.1 Turn Order & Round Flow

Each combat round follows this exact sequence:

Status Tick – all ongoing status effects apply their damage/healing and update durations.

SP Gain – the party’s shared SP pool is increased (Section 4.2).

Action Pool Draw – Four Fates Draw generates 4 random abilities.

Action Assignment – player chooses actions for each party member, constrained by SP.

Initiative Calculation – all acting units are ordered by SPD plus speed modifiers.

Action Execution – units act in order; actions may be canceled if the acting unit is incapacitated first.

Round End – leftover SP is clamped, win/loss conditions are checked.

This round structure is consistent for all encounters, including the final unwinnable fight.

4.2 Shared Stamina (SP) – “Fading Nerve”

The party uses a shared SP pool for all actions.

SP is an integer resource; fractional values are never used.

Starting SP:

At the start of every combat encounter, the party begins with 10 SP.

SP Gain Each Round:

At the start of each round (including Round 1), the party gains 6 SP.

The SP pool can never exceed 18 SP at any time; any surplus is lost.

End-of-Round Clamp (Fading Nerve):

After all actions have resolved and before the next round begins, if the party has more than 12 SP remaining, their SP is reduced to 12 SP.

This creates the following design behavior:

The player can temporarily spike SP by playing conservatively, but cannot hoard indefinitely.

To maximize efficiency, players are encouraged to spend boldly each round, rather than always saving for a single giant combo.

4.3 Initiative & Speed

Base Initiative:

Each unit’s base initiative is their SPD value.

Ability Speed Modifier:

Each ability (Standard, Special, Duo, Trio, or Party Action) has a speed modifier, which can be positive (faster), zero, or negative (slower).

Final Initiative per Round:

When a unit is assigned an action, their initiative for that round is:

Base SPD plus the ability’s speed modifier.

Sorting Rules:

Units with higher initiative number act before those with lower.

If two units have the same initiative:

Player characters act before enemies.

If two player characters tie, their order can be consistent (for example, fixed roster order) or any stable rule.

If two enemies tie, order can follow the order they were created or a stable sorting rule.

If a unit does not receive an action that round (for example, due to insufficient SP or deliberate idling), they do not appear in the initiative list.

4.4 Win/Loss Conditions

Standard Encounters:

Victory: All enemies in the encounter are incapacitated.

Defeat: All party members are incapacitated (HP at 0 or below).

Final Encounter Exception:

The Final Chamber enemies are flagged as Unwinnable.

Unwinnable enemies cannot be reduced below 1 HP.

Victory is instead achieved by surviving until the Doom Threshold triggers and successfully executing the Retreat action (details in Section 13).

5. ACTIONS & ABILITY SYSTEM
5.1 Ability Types

All player-facing abilities are classified into the following categories:

Standard Abilities

Core actions specific to each character.

Cost 3 SP in most cases.

Typically single-target attacks, defenses, or small buffs/debuffs.

Special Abilities

Stronger, signature abilities for each character.

Cost 5 SP.

Often involve powerful effects, more damage, or larger area of effect.

Duo Combos

Actions that require exactly two specific characters to participate.

Use both participants’ actions for the round.

High-impact, mid-to-high SP cost.

Trio Combos

Actions that require all three current party members.

Use all party actions for the round.

Very high impact and high SP cost; some are limited to once per combat.

Party Actions

General-purpose actions such as Use Item or Retreat.

Some Party Actions are always available; others may appear in the random action pool.

5.2 SP Costs & Role of Combos

Typical SP costs by type:

Standard: 3 SP

Special: 5 SP

Duo: usually 7 to 9 SP

Trio: usually 11 to 16 SP

Party Action: varies by action (3 SP for Use Item; 0 SP for special cases like Final Retreat once triggered).

Combos are high-commitment, high-impact tools:

They consume the actions of 2 or 3 characters in that round.

They usually provide strong damage and/or strong buffs/debuffs.

They are designed to be situationally powerful, not default choices every round.

Single-character actions remain highly attractive because they:

Are more flexible.

Allow multiple characters to act in the same round.

Often provide reliable control effects (taunt, marks, healing, regen, vulnerability, etc.).

5.3 Always-Available Actions (Last Resort Suite)

These actions are always visible and selectable during combat for each conscious character. They are not part of the random action pool.

Basic Strike

Cost: 2 SP

User: Any conscious party member

Target: Single enemy

Effect:

Deals a small, fixed amount of damage (for example, 5 damage), independent of CON and Level.

Purpose:

Provides a low-cost attack option when SP is low or the random pool is unfavorable.

Guard

Cost: 2 SP

User: Any conscious party member

Target: Self

Effect:

Reduces damage taken by that character by 40% until their next turn.

Purpose:

Allows defensive play even if defensive abilities do not appear in the random pool.

Use Item

Cost: 3 SP

User: Any conscious party member

Target: Depends on item selected

Availability:

Only available if the party has at least one usable combat item.

Effect:

Opens a sub-selection to choose an item.

Items may heal, remove debuffs, deal damage, or apply status effects.

Always-available actions ensure that the player never feels completely helpless, even with a poor random roll.

5.4 Random Action Pool – “Four Fates Draw”

Each round, the game offers the player 4 random abilities drawn from the party’s available actions.

Source Pool for Random Draw:

All Standard and Special abilities of the current three party members.

All Duo combos available for the current party composition.

All Trio combos available for the current party composition.

Any Party Actions designated as pool-based (none by default in 1.0; this is reserved for future expansion).

Role Tags:

Each ability in the source pool has one or more role tags, such as:

Offense

Support

Defense

Control

Utility

These tags are used when constructing the pool for the round.

Four Fates Draw Algorithm (Conceptual):

Guarantee at least one Offense-tagged ability in the pool, if any exist.

Guarantee at least one Support or Defense-tagged ability in the pool, if any exist.

Fill remaining slots (until there are 4 total) with random abilities from the source pool, excluding those already chosen.

No ability appears more than once in the 4-slot pool.

The previous round’s pool does not affect the current one; the draw is entirely fresh each round.

This results in a pool that is unpredictable but fair, always offering at least one offensive option and usually one stabilizing/support option, assuming such abilities are present in the party’s kits.

6. RESOLUTION RULES & FORMULAS
6.1 Damage & Healing – “Shared Blood”

Unless otherwise specified, direct damage and direct healing follow a shared formula:

Final damage or healing equals the ability’s Set Value plus a Conviction-based bonus.

The Conviction-based bonus equals the acting unit’s Conviction multiplied by Level_Value, rounded up.

In words:

Final Value = Set Value + (Conviction × Level_Value, rounded up)

For single-user abilities, the Conviction used is the acting character’s CON.

6.2 Multi-User Abilities (Duos & Trios)

For Duo and Trio combos, Conviction scaling uses the average Conviction of all participating characters:

Take the Conviction (CON) values of all participating characters.

Compute the average.

Round this average up to the nearest whole number.

Multiply this value by Level_Value for the current floor.

Round that result up to get the Conviction-based bonus term.

Final damage or healing for a combo then follows:

Final Value = Set Value + (Average Conviction × Level_Value, rounded up)

This ensures that combos benefit from both participants without exploding in power from adding multiple Conviction stats directly.

6.3 Random Chances (Status Application)

Many abilities apply status effects with a percentage chance (for example, 30% or 50%). Unless otherwise stated:

Each status application chance is an independent check, evaluated once per target per ability use.

If an ability hits multiple targets, each target rolls separately.

Status effects that “trigger when hit” (such as Spell Parry’s reflection) are resolved at the time of the triggering event.

There is no separate hit or miss system in v1.0; all attacks connect, and only status effects may be probabilistic.

7. STATUS EFFECTS – “TIDE OF DECAY”
7.1 Status Effect Fields

Every status effect instance in the game is defined by:

Status ID – a unique identifier used internally.

Display Name – player-facing name (for example, Poison, Bleed, Gravemark).

Type – one of:

Damage over Time (DoT)

Heal over Time (HoT)

Buff

Debuff

Control (stun, paralysis, etc.)

Family – a grouping key used for stacking rules. Examples:

Poison

Bleed

Burn

Regen

DamageUp

DamageDown

SPDUp

SPDDown

MagicVuln

CritUp

DamageTakenDown

EvasionUp

Magnitude – the numeric strength of the effect.

Duration – the number of rounds remaining.

Status effects are attached to individual combatants.

7.2 Stacking & Capacity Rules

To keep behavior predictable and avoid runaway stacking:

Each unit can have at most:

Three active DoT effects at the same time.

Three active HoT effects at the same time.

There is no fixed limit on Buffs, Debuffs, or Control effects, but family rules apply (below).

Same-Family Effects:

If a new status of the same family is applied to a unit that already has an active status from that family:

Compare magnitudes.

Keep the higher magnitude of the two.

Reset the duration to the new effect’s full duration.

In other words, same-family statuses do not stack in strength; they refresh and overwrite based on which is stronger.

Different-Family Effects:

Status effects with different families can stack freely.

Example: a unit can be affected by Poison, Bleed, and Burn simultaneously.

7.3 Tick Order & Resolution Priority

At the start of every combat round, before SP is gained, each unit resolves their status effects in the following order:

All DoT effects apply their damage and reduce HP accordingly.

All HoT effects apply their healing and increase HP accordingly (respecting maximum HP).

Buff, Debuff, and Control effects apply any on-tick secondary behaviors if specified. Most only modify values passively and do not have a per-tick effect.

Duration Reduction

After all effects have processed, each status effect’s duration is reduced by 1.

Cleanup

Any status effect whose duration has reached zero is removed.

This order guarantees that damage over time is applied before ongoing healing, and that a status effect always lasts for exactly the number of full rounds specified.

7.4 Common Status Families

The following are standard status families used across abilities:

Poison (DoT): Deals periodic damage; thematic focus on toxins.

Bleed (DoT): Deals periodic physical damage; often from slashes and punctures.

Burn (DoT): Deals periodic fire or magical heat damage.

Regen (HoT): Restores HP over multiple rounds.

DamageUp (Debuff/Buff): Increases damage dealt or damage taken, depending on context.

DamageDown (Debuff): Reduces damage dealt by the affected unit.

DamageTakenDown (Buff): Reduces damage taken by the affected unit.

SPDUp (Buff): Increases Speed.

SPDDown (Debuff): Reduces Speed.

MagicVuln (Debuff): Increases damage taken from magic.

CritUp (Buff): Increases chance of critical hits (if crit mechanics are used).

EvasionUp (Buff): Increases chance to avoid or reduce incoming damage, where such mechanics are implemented.

Control (e.g., Stun, Paralysis): Prevents the unit from acting for some duration.

Each named status effect in abilities is associated with one of these families, so the stacking rules are clear and consistent.

11. DUNGEON & FLOOR STRUCTURE

Floors: 4 per run.

Room Graph: Each floor is a connected graph of rooms with exactly one Start and one Exit room.

Typical room count by floor:

Floor 1: 5–7 rooms

Floor 2: 6–8 rooms

Floor 3: 7–9 rooms

Floor 4: 8–10 rooms (includes Antechamber and Final Chamber sequence)

Room types include:

Enemy

Narrative

Resource

Trap

Empty

Boss (Final Chamber)

Each room stores:

Its unique ID

Its type

Whether it has been cleared

Its links to other rooms

Event data, such as which enemy encounter, narrative event, or trap it contains.

A path from Start to Exit is always guaranteed to exist on each floor.

12. ENEMY DESIGN & SCALING – “PRESSURE CURVE”

Enemies are defined by:

Base HP, CON, SPD (for Floor 1)

Archetype (Trash, Elite, Brute, Skirmisher, Support)

Abilities (similar structure to player abilities)

Passives (such as on-death splits)

Tags (for vulnerability or synergy)

Archetypes adjust HP, Conviction, and Speed:

Trash:

CON multiplier: 0.8

SPD multiplier: 1.0

Elite:

HP multiplier: 1.4

CON multiplier: 1.2

SPD multiplier: 1.0

Brute:

HP multiplier: 1.8

CON multiplier: 1.0

Skirmisher:

HP multiplier: 0.7

CON multiplier: 0.9

SPD multiplier: 1.5

Support:

HP multiplier: 1.0

CON multiplier: 1.1

SPD multiplier: 1.0

12.2 Floor-Based Scaling

For Floor number F (1–4), scaling multipliers are:

HP multiplier from floor: 1 plus 0.25 for each floor beyond the first.

Damage scaling reference: 1 plus 0.20 for each floor beyond the first.

Conviction scaling: 1 plus 0.10 for each floor beyond the first.

Final HP for an enemy at floor F:

Base HP

Multiplied by archetype HP multiplier

Multiplied by floor HP multiplier

Rounded to the nearest integer.

Final CON is scaled similarly with archetype and floor Conviction multipliers. SPD is multiplied by the archetype SPD multiplier only and does not scale further with floor.

13. FINAL ENCOUNTER & END-GAME FLOW
13.1 Final Chamber & Unwinnable Flag

The Final Chamber is on Floor 4.

Enemies in this room are flagged as Unwinnable:

Their HP cannot be reduced below 1.

Any incoming damage that would reduce them to 0 HP instead leaves them at 1.

13.2 Doom Threshold & Retreat Logic

The fight proceeds normally until the Doom Threshold is reached:

Doom Threshold is triggered when either:

The party’s combined current HP divided by combined maximum HP drops below 30%, or

The fight has lasted 5 or more rounds.

When the Doom Threshold triggers:

A flag is set indicating the encounter is now in the final phase.

The standard Attack command in the UI is replaced by a Retreat Party Action.

At the start of the next round:

If the party’s SP is below 10, it is raised to 10.

All allies gain Retreat Ward:

They cannot be reduced below 1 HP until the Retreat action resolves.

Retreat Action:

Cost: 0 SP once the final phase begins.

Initiative: resolves before all other actions in the round.

Condition: if at least one party member is still conscious when Retreat is chosen, it always succeeds.

On success:

Combat ends.

The party is transported back to the Antechamber room.

A fixed end-game sequence plays, culminating in the Legacy Preserved screen, marking the run as a win.

If the party is wiped before triggering Doom Threshold or before Retreat is chosen, the run ends as a failure.

15. CODER CHEAT SHEET (SUMMARY)

Core Constants

Party size: 3

Floors per run: 4

Starting SP per encounter: 10

SP gain per round: 6

Maximum SP at any moment: 18

End-of-round SP clamp: maximum 12

Party level equals floor number (1–4)

Formulas

Level_Value:

Level 1: 0.5

Level 2: 0.6

Level 3: 0.7

Level 4: 0.8

Single-user abilities:

Final damage or healing = Set Value + (user CON × Level_Value, rounded up)

Multi-user (Duo/Trio) abilities:

Use average CON of participants, rounded up, instead of single CON.

Status System

Types: DoT, HoT, Buff, Debuff, Control

Max active DoTs per unit: 3

Max active HoTs per unit: 3

Same-family status: keep highest magnitude, refresh duration

Different-family statuses: stack freely

Tick order at start of round:

Apply all DoT damage

Apply all HoT healing

Resolve any on-tick Buff/Debuff/Control effects

Decrement durations

Remove any expired statuses

Initiative

Initiative = SPD + ability speed modifier

Higher initiative acts first

Ties: player characters before enemies; within each side, consistent tie-breaking rule (such as roster order)

Random Action Pool

4 abilities per round

Source: Standards, Specials, Duos, Trios, and pool-based Party Actions

At least 1 Offense-tagged ability if possible

At least 1 Support or Defense-tagged ability if possible

No duplicates in the same round

Always-available actions (Basic Strike, Guard, Use Item) sit outside the random pool

Final Encounter Rules

Unwinnable enemies cannot drop below 1 HP

Doom Threshold triggers when:

Party HP ratio < 30%, or

Round count ≥ 5

After Doom Threshold:

SP raised to at least 10 at next round start

All allies gain Retreat Ward (cannot drop below 1 HP until Retreat resolves)

Retreat is a 0 SP Party Action that acts before all others and always succeeds if at least one ally is conscious

Abilities & Combos

Single abilities are tuned so that using 2–3 of them in a round is competitive with one Duo or Trio in terms of total damage/control.

Duo damage abilities generally deliver roughly 2.2–3.0 damage per SP (before Conviction scaling).

Trio abilities range from powerful control and party-wide effects to top-end burst (Climax of Fate), with the strongest ones limited to once per combat or carrying tradeoffs (such as Exhausted Font).
